# AI THREATS – EMERGING RISKS AND REAL-WORLD INCIDENTS

## 1. Title of the Threat
**AI-Generated Deepfake Videos Used for Financial Fraud: The Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Vijay Case (September 2026)**

## 2. Description of the Incident
In September 2026, Tamil Nadu's Crime Branch-Criminal Investigation Department (CB-CID) investigated a financial fraud involving AI-generated deepfake videos impersonating Chief Minister C. Joseph Vijay.

According to news reports, the videos showed an AI-generated version of Vijay speaking in a fabricated Hindi voice and offering financial assistance. Viewers were instructed to contact a WhatsApp number to receive help. The videos circulated on Facebook and other social media platforms.

On 12 September 2026, police arrested a 28-year-old man in Rajasthan in connection with the case. Investigators also identified a person in Madhya Pradesh who had allegedly lost money through the fraud. Police continued investigating whether the accused acted independently or was connected to a larger network.

Artificial Intelligence played a central role by making the impersonation appear believable. Deepfake technology can generate or manipulate a person's facial appearance, voice and video, allowing criminals to create convincing false statements attributed to real individuals.

The incident demonstrates how AI-generated media can be combined with traditional social engineering techniques to deceive people and facilitate financial fraud.

## 3. Impact
The incident highlights the following impacts:

1. **Financial Loss:** People who trust fraudulent financial assistance offers may lose money. Police identified a person who had allegedly been cheated, although the total financial loss was not publicly established in the cited reports.
2. **Public Deception:** Viewers may believe that the Chief Minister genuinely announced a financial assistance scheme.
3. **Reputational Damage:** Impersonating a public official can undermine public confidence in that person's genuine statements.
4. **Privacy Risks:** Victims may disclose personal information, banking details or other sensitive data to fraudsters.
5. **Investigative Costs:** Police must analyse digital evidence, trace accounts and coordinate investigations across different states.
6. **Loss of Trust in Digital Media:** Convincing deepfakes make it more difficult for people to distinguish genuine videos from fabricated content.

## 4. Potential Risks
This incident reveals several emerging risks associated with AI misuse:

1. **Identity Impersonation:** Criminals can imitate public officials, business executives, celebrities or ordinary individuals.
2. **Financial Fraud:** Deepfake videos and cloned voices can be used to promote fake investment schemes, fraudulent donations and false government assistance programmes.
3. **Corporate Payment Fraud:** Attackers may impersonate senior executives during video calls and pressure employees into transferring money.
4. **Social Engineering:** Realistic AI-generated content can increase an attacker's credibility and manipulate victims into following instructions.
5. **Misinformation:** Fabricated videos of public figures can spread false information and influence public opinion.
6. **Cross-Border Cybercrime:** Criminals operating through accounts or infrastructure in different countries can complicate investigations and law enforcement.
7. **Identity Verification Challenges:** Organisations that rely only on a person's voice or appearance may become more vulnerable to impersonation.
8. **Psychological Harm:** Victims may experience stress, embarrassment and a loss of confidence in digital communication.
9. **Increased Cybersecurity Costs:** Businesses and government agencies may need additional security controls, employee training and fraud-detection systems.
10. **Reduced Public Trust:** Repeated misuse of deepfakes may cause people to question even authentic recordings and legitimate announcements.

## 5. Prevention and Mitigation
The following measures can help reduce the risks associated with AI-generated deepfakes.

### A. Individual-Level Prevention
1. **Verify information:** Confirm financial assistance schemes through official government websites and verified communication channels.
2. **Avoid suspicious contact details:** Do not contact unknown numbers or join WhatsApp groups promoted through unverified videos.
3. **Protect sensitive information:** Never disclose passwords, OTPs or banking credentials to unverified individuals.
4. **Use independent verification:** If a video or voice message requests money, confirm the request through a separate, trusted communication channel.
5. **Report suspicious content:** Report fraudulent videos to the relevant platform and cybercrime authorities.

### B. Organisation-Level Prevention
1. **Implement multi-factor verification:** Financial transactions should require appropriate authentication beyond a video call or voice confirmation.
2. **Introduce dual approval:** High-value transfers should require approval from more than one authorised employee.
3. **Train employees:** Conduct cybersecurity awareness programmes that include deepfake impersonation and social engineering scenarios.
4. **Use detection technology:** AI-based detection tools can help identify suspicious synthetic audio and video, although their results should not be treated as conclusive proof.
5. **Maintain incident-response procedures:** Organisations should preserve evidence, report incidents promptly and follow established fraud-response procedures.

### C. Platform and Government Measures
Social media platforms should strengthen the detection, reporting and removal of fraudulent impersonation content. Clear labelling and provenance information can also help users understand whether media has been artificially generated or altered.

India's February 2026 amendments to the Information Technology Rules strengthened requirements concerning harmful synthetic content, including impersonation. The government has also supported responsible AI projects involving deepfake and audio-visual forgery detection.

However, technology alone cannot eliminate this threat. Effective prevention requires cooperation between individuals, organisations, technology providers and law enforcement agencies.

## 7. Conclusion
The Tamil Nadu Chief Minister Vijay deepfake case demonstrates how Artificial Intelligence can be misused to impersonate trusted public figures and facilitate financial fraud. By combining fabricated videos, social media distribution and fraudulent contact channels, criminals can exploit people's trust for financial gain.

The incident also highlights the limitations of relying solely on visual appearance or voice as proof of identity. Individuals must verify suspicious requests independently, while organisations should implement strong transaction controls, employee training and appropriate detection systems.

> **Key Takeaway:** AI-generated videos and voices should never be treated as sufficient proof of identity. Always verify important financial requests through an independent and trusted channel.
